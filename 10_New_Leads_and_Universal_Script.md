## The Universal Script

Works on calls, Facebook messages, or in person. Swap the [brackets]; the skeleton never changes.

### Opener (10 seconds — earn the next 30)

> "Hi, is this [name]? My name's Josh — I'm local here in Navarre. Do you have literally one minute? I'm not selling anything today, I just have a quick question about [business name]."

_Why it works: "local" + "one minute" + "not selling anything today" disarms the reflex hang-up. And it's true — today you're asking a question._

### The Question (make them say the problem out loud)

> "I found you on Facebook — [genuine specific compliment: 'those before-and-after shots are great' / 'your reviews are killer']. When I tried to find your website to learn more, I couldn't. Is that on purpose, or just something that never got to the top of the list?"

_Why it works: it's a compliment plus a curiosity question, not a pitch. Nobody gets defensive about "never got around to it" — almost everyone says exactly that._

### The Bridge (their answer doesn't matter — the bridge is the same)

> "That's what I figured, and honestly it's most businesses around here. Here's the thing though — when someone Googles [their service] in Navarre, your competitors with websites show up first, even when your reviews are better. You're doing the hard part — the reputation — and they're collecting the search traffic."

### The Offer (free, zero risk, specific)

> "So here's why I called. I build simple websites for local businesses — nothing fancy, no contracts. What I'd like to do is build you a free sample homepage — your name, your photos, your reviews — so you can see it. If you hate it, you've lost nothing and I'll delete it. If you like it, we talk. Fair?"

_Why it works: "Fair?" is hard to answer with no. The ask isn't money — it's permission to show them something free._

### Objection Handling

**"I get enough business from Facebook / word of mouth."**

> "And that's exactly why I called you and not somebody struggling — a website works best for businesses that already have the reputation. It's not about replacing word of mouth, it's about catching the people word of mouth doesn't reach — folks who just moved here and Google everything."

**"I can't afford it right now."**

> "Totally get it — the sample page is free either way, and there's no clock on it. If it's not the right season, it sits there until it is. Can I at least send it over when it's done?"

**"I don't have time to deal with a website."**

> "That's the point of me — you don't touch anything. I pull your photos and reviews off your Facebook page, you look at one link and say yes or no. Your total time invested is about two minutes."

**"No thanks." (the flat no)**

> "No problem at all, thanks for hearing me out. One last thing — can I message the sample link to your Facebook page anyway once it's done? No obligation, worst case you ignore it."
> _(This is the line you wished you'd used with Nails by Mindy. A flat no to a pitch is often a yes to a free look.)_

**"They just closed / are closing."**

> "Sorry to hear that — good luck with whatever's next. Quick thought: do you know anyone else running a business around here who might want to be easier to find online?"
> _(A dead lead can still be a referral.)_

### Close (whichever branch you're on)

> "Great — I'll put it together this week and message it to your page. If you like it, we'll grab coffee or hop on a call. Thanks for your time, [name]."

### The Follow-Up Message (2–3 days after any "maybe" or soft no)

> "Hi [name], Josh here — we spoke [day]. Here's that free sample page I mentioned: [link]. No pressure at all; if it's not for you, just ignore this. If you want anything changed, that's free too. Either way, keep being awesome at [thing you complimented]."
