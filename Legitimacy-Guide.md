# Navarre Web Care — Path to Full Legitimacy

_Florida sole proprietorship path — prepared July 2026_

## Where you stand today

Website built. Business name picked ("Navarre Web Care"). Nothing registered yet, no EIN, no bank account, no license. That's a completely normal starting point — none of it stops you from working today.

## The remaining steps, in order

**1. Get an EIN — free, ~10 minutes**
Apply directly at irs.gov (search "apply for an EIN online"). Free. Instant. Do this first — no reason to wait.

**2. File your Fictitious Name (DBA) with Florida — $50**
File online at sunbiz.org (Division of Corporations). $50, good for five years, renews for $50. This is the only step in the list that costs anything close to real money, and it's what lets you legally invoice and get paid as "Navarre Web Care" instead of your own name. Florida law technically also asks you to publish a notice of intent once in a local newspaper before filing, but Sunbiz doesn't check for this in practice — a nominal cost if you choose to do it.

**3. Open a business bank account**
Bring your EIN confirmation letter and DBA registration to a bank. Keeps business and personal money separate — important for taxes and for looking legitimate to clients.

**4. Register for a Local Business Tax Receipt**
Through the Santa Rosa County Tax Collector (srctc.com), plus a city receipt too if you're inside city limits. Fee depends on business type — call their office for the exact number for a web design/service business.

**5. Check Florida sales tax registration**
Most pure web-design labor isn't subject to FL sales tax, but if you resell hosting, software, or templates as tangible/taxable items, you may need to register with the FL Department of Revenue. Worth a quick call to confirm once you know your service mix.

**6. General liability / errors & omissions insurance — optional, recommended**
Not legally required, but worth getting once you have paying clients — cheap for a solo operation (rough ballpark $25–40/month) and protects you if a client claims your work caused them a loss.

**7. Contract + invoicing system**
A simple written service agreement (scope, payment terms, revisions, ownership of the code/design) and a real invoice — even a free template from Wave or Square — makes you look and operate like an actual business regardless of what paperwork is filed yet.

## Can you cold call before any of this is done? Yes.

In Florida, you're legally allowed to operate as a sole proprietor and pick up the phone today under your own legal name — no registration required to _start working_. The DBA is only required once you want to hold yourself out, invoice, or get paid under the name "Navarre Web Care" specifically.

Practical path:

- Start calling today, for free.
- When someone says yes, you can paper the deal and invoice under your own legal name, or simply get the $50 DBA filed (it's instant online) before you send the first invoice under the business name.
- Use the deposit/first payment to cover the $50 filing fee. Totally normal way to bootstrap.

One thing to avoid: don't take checks made out to "Navarre Web Care" or advertise for paid work under that name before the DBA is filed — operating under an unregistered fictitious name is technically a low-level misdemeanor under Florida Statute 865.09, though enforcement against solo freelancers is essentially unheard of. Easiest fix: file the $50 DBA the same day you land your first client, before money changes hands under the business name.

## Cold-calling rules that actually matter

- **B2B calls are exempt from the National Do Not Call Registry.** You're pitching businesses on a business service, so most of your calls fall under this exemption.
- **Manual dialing is fine.** The exemption doesn't cover auto-dialers/robocalls to cell phones without consent (that's TCPA territory) — but a person manually dialing prospects, which is what you'd be doing, is standard and legal.
- **Call 8am–9pm local time only.**
- **Honor a business owner's do-not-call request even though you're technically exempt** — reduces complaint risk for basically no cost to you.
- **Florida's own Telemarketing Act** mainly targets consumer-facing "commercial telephone sellers." Straightforward B2B outreach for services generally isn't treated as regulated telemarketing requiring a license, but if you ever scale into bulk/high-volume calling campaigns, a quick call to the FL Dept. of Agriculture & Consumer Services (which administers it) is worth it to confirm you're still clear.

## Budget snapshot

| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| EIN                           | $0                                            |
| DBA (Fictitious Name, Sunbiz) | $50, every 5 years                            |
| Local Business Tax Receipt    | Varies — call Santa Rosa County Tax Collector |
| Business bank account         | Usually free                                  |

## Suggested order of operations

1. Get your EIN today (free).
2. Start prospecting / cold calling today (free) — no need to wait on paperwork.
3. Land a client, collect a deposit.
4. File the $50 DBA that same day, before invoicing under the business name.
5. Open the business bank account.
6. Register for the county business tax receipt.
7. Add insurance once revenue supports it.
8. Consider an LLC later if you want liability protection beyond what a sole proprietorship gives you.
